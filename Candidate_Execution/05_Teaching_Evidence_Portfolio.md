## 3. Planning & STEM Integration Evidence

### Artifact 1: Primary Light Refraction STEM Unit
* **Title:** Inquiry-Based Light Refraction Lesson Sequence
* **Purpose:** Mapped to Key Stage 2 British National Curriculum Science standards, teaching wave refraction through active experiment rather than lecture.
* **Context:** Bilingual Year 5/6 classroom with diverse language backgrounds.
* **Student Age:** Years 5–6 (Ages 10–11)
* **Competencies Demonstrated:** Curriculum Planning, STEM Integration, EAL Scaffolds.
* **Supporting Evidence:** Complete lesson slides, hands-on experiment worksheets, and vocabulary card templates [C-008, C-011].
* **Reflection:** 
  - *Decision & Execution:* Replaced heavy textbook text with a physical experiment using prisms and flashlights, letting students observed reflection angles directly. Technical vocabulary was introduced *after* the observation.
  - *Next Steps:* In future units, I would coordinate with the art department to build cross-curricular lessons on color mixing.

## 4. Assessment & Collaboration Evidence

### Artifact 2: Grade 5 English Moderation Anchor Sheets
* **Title:** Standardized Writing Moderation Portfolio
* **Purpose:** Standardizing marking criteria across a team of 6 bilingual and international educators to align grading.
* **Context:** Grade 5 English writing assessment planning cycle.
* **Student Age:** Year 5 (Ages 9–10)
* **Competencies Demonstrated:** Assessment for Learning, Teacher Collaboration, Moderation.
* **Supporting Evidence:** Anonymized student writing samples, mapped rubrics, and consensus grading logs [C-013].
* **Reflection:**
  - *Decision & Execution:* Introduced "blind grading" of anonymous papers to eliminate personal teacher bias, anchoring discussions entirely on rubric descriptors.
  - *Next Steps:* I want to develop a student-friendly checklist of this rubric so that pupils can moderate their own peer work.

## 5. Differentiation & EAL Integration Evidence

### Artifact 3: Phonics Blending Scaffolding Sequence
* **Title:** Interactive Word-Blending tactile progression
* **Purpose:** Transitioning EAL students from playground communication (BICS) to phonetic reading and decoding (CALP).
* **Context:** Early Years phonics instruction block [C-019].
* **Student Age:** Early Years / Year 1 (Ages 5–6)
* **Competencies Demonstrated:** EAL Instruction, Phonics, Differentiation.
* **Supporting Evidence:** Visual phoneme blending bucket logs, letters-and-sounds checklists, and parent home-blending briefs [E-3004].
* **Reflection:**
  - *Decision & Execution:* Shifted from spelling worksheets to tactile buckets containing physical items matching target sounds. Students had to feel the object, sound it out, and write the phoneme.
  - *Next Steps:* Implement digital voice recorders so students can record their blending and self-correct.

## 6. Professional Learning & Research Evidence

### Artifact 4: Questioning Strategy CPD Action Research Brief
* **Title:** University of Cumbria Practitioner Study Summary
* **Purpose:** Investigating how structured wait-times and dialogic questioning expand student participation.
* **Context:** Practitioner action research conducted during Cumbria PGCE primary training [E-2001, C-007].
* **Student Age:** Primary (Ages 5–11)
* **Competencies Demonstrated:** Reflection, Action Research, Professional Growth.
* **Supporting Evidence:** Graded research summary brief, questioning checklists, and audio observation transcripts [E-0013].
* **Reflection:**
  - *Decision & Execution:* Proved that a standard 1-2 second teacher wait-time filters out non-native speakers. By training myself to maintain a strict 8-second pause, participation from EAL learners increased.
  - *Next Steps:* Share this wait-time framework as a school-wide CPD workshop during staff induction.
