## 1. What I Believe
I believe that **learning is an active process of collaborative inquiry, where language acquisition is a prerequisite for conceptual mastery** rather than an isolated subject. In primary education, children succeed when they are empowered to explore concepts through talk, structured hands-on inquiry, and collaboration. As an educator, my duty is to scaffold instruction so that all learners—regardless of their starting language proficiency—can access, discuss, and master high-level academic content.

---

## 2. What Students Experience in My Classroom
In my classroom, students do not passively copy notes; they are active investigators. They experience:
* **A Safe Environment for Risk-Taking:** A supportive classroom culture where verbal mistakes are valued as necessary steps toward clarity.
* **Talk Before Writing:** Opportunities to construct, debate, and verify ideas with peers through structured dialogic talk before they are asked to record them.
* **Guided Independence:** Clear, scaffolded structures that gradually fall away as pupils master concepts and take ownership of their learning.
* **Assessment as a Roadmap:** Frequent, low-stakes formative feedback that clearly guides their next learning steps.

---

## 3. What It Looks Like in Practice
This student-centered philosophy is driven by three daily practices:
* **Structured Dialogic Talk & Wait-Times:** I apply a deliberate 8-second pause after questioning. This gives all students time to process ideas, translate thoughts, and construct speech, preventing a few high-proficiency speakers from dominating discussions.
* **Cross-Curricular STEM Inquiry:** Leveraging my Physics background [E-2006], I design science and math lessons where students work physically with concepts (e.g., refraction angles or spatial fractions) using visual models and concrete objects, making abstract ideas accessible to everyone.
* **Fair, Diagnostic Assessment:** I design assessments that evaluate conceptual understanding (using labeled diagrams or classification grids) separately from a student's English writing speed.

---

## 4. How I Collaborate with Colleagues
Outstanding primary education requires joint planning and teaching consistency. I support my colleagues by:
* **Co-Planning Scaffolds:** Partnering with mainstream homeroom teachers to build language support directly into the core science and math lessons, rather than relying on pull-out interventions.
* **Assessment Moderation:** Leading collaborative moderation sessions [C-013] where we evaluate student writing samples against shared rubrics, aligning grading standards across department teams.

---

## 5. Supporting Evidence
* **Practitioner Research (University of Cumbria PGCE) [E-2001]:** Conducted a classroom study analyzing how structured teacher questioning patterns directly expand the circle of participation for lower-proficiency and quieter students.
* **Moderation Implementation (Aoxin International School) [E-3005]:** Aligned grading standards across bilingual and foreign teaching teams, reducing grading variance to under 5% and improving assessment consistency.

---

## 6. How My Thinking Has Evolved
Early in my career, I viewed EAL as a separate department task—a discrete "pull-out" block for language training. 

Over 11+ years in the classroom, my thinking evolved to focus on the **whole child and the whole class**. I now recognize that EAL strategies—such as wait-times, dual-coding, and peer talk—are not just "support strategies" for language learners. They represent **high-quality teaching for all pupils**. When we scaffold language, we clarify concepts for every child in the room.
