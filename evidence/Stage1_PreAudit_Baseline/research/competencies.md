# Professional Competencies - PGCE7002 Practitioner Research

## Overview

Competency assessments from Phase 2, rated honestly with evidence thresholds. Each competency includes evidence source, strength, confidence level, and recruiter value. Competencies marked with ❌ should NOT be claimed from this study alone.

## High Confidence Competencies (✅ Usable Now)

### 1. Professional Inquiry
- **Evidence**: Independent identification of classroom problem, formulation of research questions, mixed-methods design, ethical approval, graded output (75/100)
- **Strength**: Strong — verified by Master's-level pass with named marker
- **Confidence**: High
- **Usable Now**: ✅ Strongest asset in the whole library
- **Recruiter Value**: "I identified a genuine classroom equity problem and investigated it systematically" — fully backed by graded, institutionally verified work
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 1 §3.2; Phase 7-8; Phase 12
- **Approved Wording**: 
  - *"Independently identified a genuine classroom equity issue from practice and translated it into three specific, researchable questions."*
  - *"Independently designed and conducted a seven-participant practitioner study from an observed classroom equity issue to a graded research output."*

### 2. Reflective Practice / Critical Reflection
- **Evidence**: Sustained use of reflexive memoing with specific dated example, explicit description of changed classroom practice
- **Strength**: Strong — well above typical PGCE-level reflection; shows methodological self-scrutiny
- **Confidence**: High
- **Usable Now**: ✅
- **Recruiter Value**: Answers "tell me about a time you changed your practice based on evidence" with concrete, dated, honest example
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 1 §3.1 (memo 28 Feb 2025); Phase 2 §4; Phase 7 Reflection; Phase 6 Story 4
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Documented and managed insider-researcher bias through reflexive memoing during data collection."*
  - *"This study shifted how I respond to silence in my own classroom. When a student does not answer, I now pause and ask myself whether they might be thinking in Chinese."*

### 3. Pedagogical Evaluation
- **Evidence**: Critical evaluation of Alexander's (2020) dialogic teaching model against real classroom data
- **Strength**: Strong — genuine literature-grounded critique of established framework
- **Confidence**: High
- **Usable Now**: ✅
- **Recruiter Value**: Shows capacity to evaluate whether widely-adopted frameworks fit specific contexts
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §11; Phase 4 Finding 2 academic wording; Phase 7 Lessons Learned
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Critically evaluated a widely-used dialogic teaching framework (Alexander, 2020) against classroom data, identifying a specific limitation for linguistically diverse settings."*
  - *"Critically evaluated a leading dialogic teaching framework (Alexander, 2020) for its limitations in linguistically diverse classroom contexts."*

### 4. Evidence-Informed Teaching
- **Evidence**: Critical application of substantial theoretical base (Alexander 2020, Cummins 1984, Argyris & Schön 1974, Borg 2019, Rowe 1974, Ingram & Elliott 2016, Garcia & Wei 2014) to interrogate real classroom practice
- **Strength**: Strong — direct, sustained, critical engagement with literature
- **Confidence**: High
- **Usable Now**: ✅
- **Recruiter Value**: Demonstrates critical research evaluation rather than uncritical framework application
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §1; Phase 1 Literature links; Phase 7 Professional Growth
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Critically applied research literature to interrogate real classroom practice rather than describing theory abstractly."*
  - *"Applied Cummins's BICS–CALP framework to identify a specific risk in how bilingual learners' understanding can be mis-assessed."*

### 5. Data Analysis
- **Evidence**: Two-pass thematic coding with explicit reliability threshold, triangulation across three data types
- **Strength**: Strong — documented, auditable process with stated reliability rule
- **Confidence**: High
- **Usable Now**: ✅
- **Recruiter Value**: Transferable to any school role requiring data-informed decision-making (pupil progress, surveys, self-evaluation)
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §7; Phase 1 §D.6 (Reliability); Phase 1 §D.7 (Validity/credibility)
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Applied a two-pass thematic coding process (Braun & Clarke, 2006) with a stated reliability threshold."*
  - *"Triangulated questionnaire, interview, and thematic-coding data with a documented reliability threshold across independent sources."*

## Moderate Confidence Competencies (⚠️ Usable with Precise Framing)

### 6. Assessment Literacy (Research Instrument Sense)
- **Evidence**: Designed mixed-methods instrument to allow open-ended responses to explain numerical patterns; explicit rejection of inferential stats given n=7
- **Strength**: Moderate-strong — assessment literacy applied to research instrument design (not pupil assessment)
- **Confidence**: Moderate (Strong for research-instrument literacy; insufficient for pupil-assessment literacy specifically)
- **Usable Now**: ✅ with precise framing
- **Recruiter Value**: Shows methodological restraint — unlikely to misrepresent data to leadership
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §2; Phase 1 §E (Quantitative Findings disclaimer); Phase 1 §D.5 (Questionnaires)
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Designed a mixed-methods research instrument specifically to allow open-ended responses to explain and qualify numerical patterns."*
  - *"Demonstrated methodological restraint by explicitly declining to claim statistical significance from a small sample."*
- **Rejected Wording**: 
  - ~~"Achieved statisticallyifically significant findings"~~
  - ~~"Proved [X] through data-driven research"~~

### 7. Student-Centred Practice
- **Evidence**: Central argument that open-ended questioning (marketed as student-centred/inclusive) may not function that way for every student in linguistically uneven classroom
- **Strength**: Moderate-strong — critical thinking about what genuinely centres all students
- **Confidence**: Moderate-high
- **Usable Now**: ✅ with precise framing
- **Recruiter Value**: Distinguishes from candidates using "student-centred" as unexamined buzzword
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §12; Phase 1 Finding 2; Phase 7 Lessons Learned
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Critically examined whether a technique marketed as 'inclusive' actually functions that way for every learner in a specific context."*
  - *"Applied Cummins's BICS–CALP framework to distinguish genuine non-understanding from language-production barriers in classroom questioning."*
- **Recommended Framing**: Evidence of critical thinking about student-centredness, NOT claiming "I use student-centred methods"

### 8. Continuous Improvement
- **Evidence**: Documented personal practice change (pausing to consider Chinese thinking before assuming non-understanding)
- **Strength**: Moderate — genuine specific behaviour change, but single instance, not sustained programme
- **Confidence**: Moderate
- **Usable Now**: ✅ as small, honest example
- **Recruiter Value**: Good STAR-style example; should not be inflated into leading CI initiatives
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §13; Phase 1 Conclusion; Phase 6 Story 3 Reflection
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"This study shifted how I respond to silence in my own classroom. When a student does not answer, I now pause and ask myself whether they might be thinking in Chinese."*
- **Rejected Wording**: 
  - ~~"I lead continuous improvement initiatives"~~
  - ~~"Implemented school-wide improvement cycle"~~

### 9. Differentiation
- **Evidence**: Critical analysis of Teacher B's grouping practice (open-ended for stronger English speakers, closed for weaker) as double-edged strategy
- **Strength**: Moderate — critiquing someone else's practice as equity issue, not demonstrating own technique expertise
- **Confidence**: Moderate
- **Usable Now**: ⚠️ only as equity-critique, NOT technique-expertise
- **Recruiter Value**: Useful for demonstrating equity-conscious thinking about differentiation
- **Canonical Sources**: Phase 2 §6; Phase 1 Finding 2 analysis
- **Approved Wording**:
  - *"Critically analysed differentiation practices as an equity issue, identifying that weaker students receive less exposure to higher-order thinking questions."*
- **Rejected Wording**: 
  - ~~"I differentiate lessons using [X] method"~~
  - ~~"Expert in differentiation techniques"~~

## Evidence Hierarchy for Competency Claims

1. **Strongest**: Professional Inquiry, Reflective Practice, Pedagogical Evaluation, Evidence-Informed Teaching, Data Analysis
2. **Usable with Framing**: Assessment Literacy (research instrument), Student-Centred Practice, Continuous Improvement

## Approved vs. Rejected Wording Summary

### ✅ Approved Examples
- "Independently identified a genuine classroom equity issue from practice and translated it into three specific, researchable questions."
- "Applied Cummins's BICS–CALP framework to identify a specific risk in how bilingual learners' understanding can be mis-assessed."
- "Documented and managed insider-researcher bias through reflexive memoing during data collection."
- "Critically evaluated a widely-used dialogic teaching framework (Alexander, 2020) against classroom data, identifying a specific limitation for linguistically diverse settings."
- "Designed and conducted a mixed-methods practitioner study combining a 7-participant questionnaire with semi-structured interviews."
- "This study shifted how I respond to silence in my own classroom. When a student does not answer, I now pause and ask myself whether they might be thinking in Chinese."

### ❌ Rejected Examples
- "Achieved statistically significant findings" (n=7 too small)
- "Solved a school-wide teaching problem" (diagnosed, not solved)
- "Led a school-wide research initiative" (surveyed/interviewed colleagues, didn't lead them)
- "Achieved a distinction in postgraduate research" (75/100 is strong pass, not confirmed distinction)
- "Proved that open-ended questioning is exclusionary" (interpretivist design cannot prove causal claims)
- "I lead continuous improvement initiatives" (single documented instance only)
