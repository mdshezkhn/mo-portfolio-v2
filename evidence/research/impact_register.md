## Reading This Register Honestly

The pattern across all 7 rows is consistent: **this research is strong on diagnostic insight, methodological rigour, and critical reflection — and weak-to-absent on measured student impact and school-level implementation.**

This accurately describes what a single-school, self-report, no-observation practitioner inquiry can produce.

### Value Proposition for Recruiters
This research demonstrates: "A teacher who investigates real problems carefully and thinks critically about research" — NOT "a teacher who has proven measurable pupil outcomes from this specific piece of work."

## Impact Categories Explained

### Educational Impact
- **High**: Changes how teaching practice should be approached
- **Moderate**: Provides useful insights or considerations
- **Low**: Minimal educational relevance
- **Evidence Insufficient**: No claim can be made

### Student Impact
- **Measured**: Based on actual pupil outcome data
- **Proxy**: Teacher reports or indirect indicators
- **Evidence Insufficient**: No pupil data collected
- **N/A**: Not applicable to this study

### Teacher Impact
- **Direct Evidence**: Documented change in teacher practice or understanding
- **Indirect Evidence**: Knowledge gained that could influence practice
- **Evidence Insufficient**: No documented impact on teachers

### School Impact
- **Implemented**: Action taken at school level based on findings
- **Actionable**: Findings could inform school decisions
- **Evidence Insufficient**: No school-level action taken
- **N/A**: Not applicable

## Limitations Summary

1. **Self-report bias**: All data is teacher-perception, not independently observed
2. **Sample size**: n=7 too small for generalisation or statistical inference
3. **Single site**: Findings context-specific to one bilingual school
4. **No observation**: Classroom behaviour not directly measured
5. **No pupil data**: Cannot assess actual student outcomes
6. **Interview limitation**: Only 2 of 7 participants interviewed

## Conclusion

This research provides **diagnostic insights** and **methodological demonstration** rather than **implementation evidence**. The findings are valuable for:
- Informing CPD design
- Identifying systemic gaps
- Shaping assessment policy
- Demonstrating rigorous inquiry methods

They do NOT provide evidence for:
- Measured pupil outcomes
- Implemented school changes
- Statistical generalisation
- Action research cycles
