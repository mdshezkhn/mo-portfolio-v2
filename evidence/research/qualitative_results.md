# Qualitative Results - PGCE7002 Practitioner Research

## Methodology

Two-pass thematic coding (Braun & Clarke, 2006):
- **Pass 1**: Open coding for surface patterns
- **Pass 2**: Interpretive pass for higher-order analytical categories
- **Reliability rule**: Theme retained only if evidence appears in ≥2 independent sources

## Data Sources

| Source | Count | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire (open-ended) | 7 | Teacher responses |
| Semi-structured interviews | 2 | Teacher A, Teacher B |
| Reflexive memos | Multiple | Researcher bias documentation |

## Theme 1: Participation Filter Effect

### Evidence
- 5 of 7 teachers described open-ended questions being answered by the same few pupils
- Both interviewees (A and B) confirmed the pattern

### Key Quotes

**Teacher A (Paraphrase)**: Described resorting to closed questions under time and pacing pressure, stating that they are safer for keeping the lesson moving when managing a class of thirty pupils.

**Teacher B (Paraphrase)**: Stated that open-ended questions in English tend to be answered consistently by the same three or four English-proficient students, while the rest remain silent or look to peers.

**Teacher A (Paraphrase)**: (Unable to verify CPD context or quotation verbatim from raw interview notes. Removed direct quotation. Notes show Teacher A generally observed that open-ended questions can cause participation breakdown for quieter students.)

### Analytical Interpretation
Open-ended questioning, in this bilingual context, functioned as a participation filter rather than an inclusion strategy. The format itself favored English production over comprehension.

---

## Theme 2: Language Production vs. Conceptual Understanding

### Evidence
- 3 of 7 teachers (A, B, E) independently described pupils who understood concepts in Chinese but couldn't articulate answers in English
- Teacher B admitted categorising students as "not getting it" due to language barriers

### Key Quotes

**Teacher A (Paraphrase)**: Noted that some students clearly demonstrate conceptual understanding when collaborating in Chinese, but remain silent or unable to respond when questioned in English.

**Teacher B (Paraphrase)**: Reflected on the difficulty of admitting that language production barriers (BICS/CALP) could sometimes lead to reading a student as lacking understanding when they simply could not formulate the English response.

### Analytical Interpretation
Consistent with Cummins's (1984) BICS–CALP distinction — conversational fluency develops faster than academic language needed to demonstrate understanding aloud. English-medium questioning risks conflating language production with conceptual understanding.

---

## Theme 3: Absence of Institutional Framework

### Evidence
- Universal absence across all 7 questionnaires and both interviews
- No teacher referenced any shared questioning framework or school policy

### Analytical Interpretation
Every teacher was independently solving the same problem through trial and error. This structural gap explains why the belief–practice gap persists even among self-aware teachers.

---

## Theme 4: Mixed Wait-Time Effects

### Evidence
- Teacher B: Extended pauses produced more hands and better answers (consistent with Rowe, 1974)
- Teacher F: Same stillness was read by pupils as "something has gone wrong," not productive thinking time

### Analytical Interpretation
Wait-time effects are context-dependent, not universally beneficial. Individual teacher interpretation of silence varies significantly.

---

## Theme 5: Self-Monitoring and Recalibration

### Evidence
- Teacher A displayed self-monitoring behaviour mid-interview
- Initially emphasised open-ended questions as most valued
- Shifted toward affirming closed questions
- Researcher's analytical memo flagged this as possibly reflecting awareness of being evaluated rather than settled conviction

### Analytical Interpretation
Teachers may adjust their responses when aware of evaluation, suggesting self-report data requires careful interpretation.

---

## Negative Findings (Absence)

### No Formal Framework Referenced
Not one of the 7 teachers referenced:
- A formal questioning framework
- A school-level policy on questioning in bilingual lessons
- Any shared institutional guidance on managing language diversity

This absence finding held consistently across both questionnaire and interview data.
