## Evidence Hierarchy

> **Principle: "Missing from repository" does not equal "does not exist." The hierarchy below measures the quality and independence of evidence — not whether a document has been uploaded to a specific folder yet.**

| Level | Meaning | Example | Public Use |
|-------|---------|---------|------------|
| **A** | Independently verifiable documentary evidence — exists independently of your own account | Degree certificate, employment letter, published transcript, published research paper | Safe everywhere — highest confidence |
| **B** | Documentary evidence created or issued contemporaneously by a third party and under your control | PGCE submission, employment correspondence, qualification certificate you hold, lesson observation note, employer-issued documents | Acceptable for public use with factual wording |
| **C** | Single-source assertion — your account is the only source; no corroborating document | Personal statement about a role's scope, unverified metrics, recalled experience without documentation | Requires supporting evidence before public use |
| **D** | Aspirational — future goals, career preferences, ambitions | Target regions, career direction, intended qualifications | Must never appear as current achievement |

**The key distinction between B and C:** A Level B claim is one where a third party issued a document — even if that document is in your possession rather than this repository. A Level C claim is one where your own recollection or assertion is the only source.

> **Note on CVs:** A CV is a **derived document** — a summary that points to evidence. It is not itself evidence. A CV entry for a qualification is supported by the certificate (Level B). A CV entry for a role is supported by the employment letter (Level A once received). Citing "CV" as the sole source of a claim does not make that claim Level B.

## Claim Lifecycle

Every achievement or claim must move through these stages before appearing in any public asset. No stage may be skipped.

```
Idea  (career thought or aspiration)
  ↓
Observed  (experience happened — you were there)
  ↓
Documented  (third-party document exists or was created at the time)
  ↓
Verified  (primary document filed; Evidence Register entry confirmed)
  ↓
Approved  (entered in Traceability Matrix; Evidence Quality A or B; Public Confidence Medium or High)
  ↓
Published  (appears in CV, LinkedIn, portfolio, cover letter, or interview materials)
```

**Lifecycle rules:**
- *Observed* stage → Level C. Defensible in interview but must not be published.
- *Documented* stage → Level B. May be published with careful, measured wording.
- *Verified* stage → Level A. May be published without qualification.
- A claim that regresses (e.g. a metric is corrected downward) must be withdrawn from all public assets immediately and the Change Log updated.
- **Inflation is irreversible reputational damage. When in doubt, use the more conservative claim.**
