The Philosophy

Great teaching is not about the transmission of facts, but the ignition of inquiry. It requires a delicate balance of compassion and academic rigor. When students feel seen and supported, they are empowered to take the intellectual risks necessary for true growth.

My practice is anchored in evidence. I rely on formative assessment, structured differentiation, and dialogic teaching to ensure that every voice in the classroom is heard and valued.

Yet, beyond the data and frameworks, my ultimate goal is to nurture independent thinkers who approach the world with curiosity and resilience.

Signature Lesson Walkthrough

Cross-Curricular STEM & Literacy

Combining my B.Sc. in Physics with language acquisition theory, I design lessons that anchor abstract vocabulary to physical phenomena. Students don't just memorise words; they experience the concepts.

Lesson Context

Subject: EAL / Science Integration

Objective: Applying scientific vocabulary to describe states of matter.

Students engaged in a hands-on experiment, using inquiry-based questioning to articulate their hypotheses. Language frames were provided to support bilingual learners in formulating complex sentences.

Assessment Approach

Assessment is a tool for learning, not just a summary of it. I utilize continuous formative feedback loops to adjust instruction dynamically. By analyzing student outputs and participation metrics, I identify linguistic gaps and intervene before they become barriers.

AI & Technology

Technology must amplify, not replace, great teaching. I integrate modern pedagogical tools and AI-driven platforms to provide personalized language practice, automate routine data collection, and free up instructional time for high-value dialogic interactions.

Reflection

Every lesson is an iteration. The modern classroom requires an educator who is as much a researcher as they are an instructor, constantly refining the learning experience based on tangible outcomes and student voice.
