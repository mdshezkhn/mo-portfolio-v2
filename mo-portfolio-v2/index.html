My Story

My teaching career began in Mumbai before taking me to mainland China in 2016.

My Journey

Since then, my experience has spanned early years through upper primary, giving me a broad understanding of learners across developmental stages. After teaching in Shenzhen and Huzhou, I found my professional home in Zhengzhou's bilingual international schools, where I have spent the majority of my career teaching the full primary grade range. My work has centred on English as an Additional Language (EAL), using language as a bridge to Science, Mathematics, and cross-curricular inquiry so that multilingual learners develop both academic English and deep conceptual understanding. Alongside my classroom responsibilities, I have led Grade 5 writing moderation and mentored newly appointed teachers.

Research-informed Practice

My practitioner research for the PGCE, University of Cumbria examined how questioning strategies facilitate high-quality teaching and participation equity in bilingual classrooms. That work, grounded in Alexander's dialogic teaching framework and translanguaging theory, changed how I plan and run discussions. I now build structured wait time and open-ended questioning sequences into lessons deliberately, not as an afterthought – and I can see the difference in which students engage.

Leadership Beyond the Classroom

Beyond my own classroom, I have demonstrated the capacity to elevate instructional quality. At WhiteHat Jr, I conducted quality assurance reviews for educators, using LMS analytics to identify teaching inconsistencies and recommend targeted improvements. At GEDU Global Education, I delivered international professional development, designing and facilitating pedagogical training for trainers across campuses in the UK, Dubai, and Malta. Those roles reinforced that good teaching and good coaching require the same discipline: specific feedback, clear standards, and honest accountability.

Looking Ahead

I hold a Postgraduate Certificate in Education (PGCE, non-QTS) from the University of Cumbria and a B.Ed from the University of Kashmir; I am not currently pursuing Qualified Teacher Status. My B.Sc. in Physics directly supports the cross-curricular STEM integration work I bring to primary classrooms. I am seeking an August 2027 position in an international or UK-aligned school where I can contribute to curriculum development and instructional leadership.

Teaching Philosophy

How I Teach & Lead

"Language is not a barrier to learning – it is the bridge. When we design for multilingual learners first, we design better for everyone."

Dialogic Teaching

Grounded in Alexander's framework, I structure classrooms around high-quality talk – structured wait time, open-ended questioning, and cumulative dialogue where ideas build on each other. This shifts participation from the confident few to the whole class.

Translanguaging as Pedagogy

Students' full linguistic repertoires are assets, not obstacles. I design lessons where learners move fluidly between languages to construct meaning, develop academic English, and demonstrate conceptual understanding in science and mathematics.

STEM Integration Through Inquiry

My physics background informs a 5E-model approach (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate) where language development and scientific reasoning advance together – not in sequence.

Assessment for Learning

Formative assessment is embedded in every lesson: hinge questions, exit tickets, and student self-assessment against co-constructed criteria. This makes learning visible to both teacher and learner.

Practitioner Research

Evidence-Informed Practice

Questioning Strategies for Participation Equity in Bilingual Classrooms

PGCE Practitioner Research • University of Cumbria • 2025–2026

This study examined how structured questioning sequences – combining extended wait time, open-ended prompts, and dialogic follow-ups – affect participation patterns among multilingual learners in primary bilingual settings.

Key implications: wait time must be explicitly taught and modelled; question sequences should be pre-planned, not improvised; and translanguaging scaffolds enable learners to rehearse ideas in stronger language before expressing in English.
