# Recruiter Translation Matrix - PGCE7002 Practitioner Research

## Overview

Each finding translated for different audiences. Every wording variant stays within what the evidence actually supports.

## Finding 1: Structural Belief–Practice Gap

### Academic Wording
Five of seven teachers demonstrated a mismatch between espoused belief (Q4A) and reported practice (Q4B) regarding questioning strategy, consistent with Argyris and Schön's (1974) distinction between espoused theory and theory-in-use.

### Professional Wording
Identified that most teachers in a bilingual school valued open-ended, higher-order questioning in principle but reverted to closed, whole-class questioning in practice — and traced this to institutional conditions (class size, pacing pressure, absent policy), not a knowledge gap.

### CV Wording
*Conducted practitioner research identifying a school-wide gap between teachers' stated beliefs and reported classroom practice around questioning strategy.*

### LinkedIn Wording
I ran a small practitioner study in my own bilingual school and found something that surprised me: teachers overwhelmingly valued open-ended questioning — but almost all of them fell back on closed, whole-class questions once the lesson was actually running. The gap wasn't about knowledge. It was about conditions.

### Interview Wording
"I noticed teachers — myself included — talking confidently about open-ended questioning, but lessons kept defaulting to closed, pace-driven exchanges. When I actually surveyed and interviewed colleagues, five of seven showed that same gap. It wasn't that people didn't know better — there was simply no shared framework or school guidance to make the harder practice feel manageable under pressure."

### Leadership Wording
Surfaced a structural, not individual, gap between stated pedagogical values and classroom practice — the kind of finding that reframes a CPD problem from "retrain the teacher" to "fix the conditions and provide shared guidance."

### Portfolio Wording
*A finding from my own PGCE practitioner research: most teachers in my school valued dialogic questioning, but classroom practice consistently defaulted to closed, whole-class exchanges — not from lack of belief, but from the absence of any shared framework to support the harder practice under real classroom pressure.*

## Finding 2: Open-Ended Questioning as Participation Filter

### Academic Wording
In teacher accounts, open-ended questioning functioned as a participation filter rather than an inclusion strategy in a linguistically uneven bilingual classroom, challenging the universalist assumptions of Alexander's (2020) dialogic teaching framework.

### Professional Wording
Found that a technique widely assumed to be more inclusive (open-ended questioning) can, in a bilingual classroom with uneven English proficiency, systematically favour the same confident English speakers while quieter pupils — some of whom understood the material perfectly well in Chinese — go unheard.

### CV Wording
*Identified, through practitioner research, that open-ended questioning can unintentionally narrow rather than widen pupil participation in linguistically diverse classrooms.*

### LinkedIn Wording
Here's a finding from my own classroom research that changed how I think about "inclusive" teaching techniques: open-ended questioning, in a bilingual classroom, tended to be answered by the same three or four English-confident pupils every time. Everyone else went quiet — not because they didn't understand, but because the format itself favoured English production over comprehension.

### Interview Wording
"Everyone assumes open-ended questions are more inclusive because they're more open. What I found, talking to colleagues in my own school, was the opposite in a bilingual setting — the same English-confident pupils answered every time, while others who clearly understood the material in Chinese stayed silent. That's a real gap between the theory and what actually happens in a linguistically mixed room."

### Leadership Wording
Provided evidence that a widely-adopted pedagogical framework (dialogic teaching) requires deliberate adaptation for linguistically diverse settings — directly relevant to any CPD or curriculum decision involving that framework in an EAL/bilingual context.

### Portfolio Wording
*My research found that open-ended questioning — usually assumed to be the more inclusive choice — actually narrowed classroom participation in a bilingual setting, consistently favouring the most English-confident pupils. This has shaped how I think about questioning design in my own multilingual classroom.*

## Finding 3: English-Medium Questioning and Cognitive Visibility (BICS–CALP)

### Academic Wording
Multiple teachers described students who demonstrated conceptual understanding in Chinese but could not produce corresponding answers in English, consistent with Cummins's (1984) BICS–CALP distinction.

### Professional Wording
Surfaced a risk that matters for any EAL/bilingual assessment practice: pupils can be silently mis-assessed as "not understanding" when the real barrier is English production, not comprehension.

### CV Wording
*Highlighted, through original research, a risk in EAL classrooms whereby English-only questioning can conflate language production with conceptual understanding.*

### LinkedIn Wording
The most important thing I found in my own research wasn't about technique — it was about assessment. Multiple teachers in my study described pupils who clearly understood a concept in Chinese, but couldn't yet say it in English — and were, in the moment, read as "not getting it." That's not a knowledge gap. That's a language-production gap being mistaken for one.

### Interview Wording
"The finding I keep coming back to is about assessment, not teaching technique. Several teachers told me about pupils who understood something perfectly in Chinese but went blank when asked in English — and admitted they'd sometimes read that as the pupil not understanding. That's a real risk for how we assess bilingual learners' progress, and it's shaped how I think about checking for understanding in my own classroom now."

### Leadership Wording
Identifies a concrete, actionable input for school assessment policy regarding EAL/bilingual learners — specifically, the need to distinguish "understands but can't yet say in English" from genuine non-understanding in formative assessment practice.

### Portfolio Wording
*Perhaps the most consequential finding from my practitioner research: teachers repeatedly described pupils who understood content in Chinese but could not yet produce an English answer — and some admitted this had, at times, been read as a lack of understanding. This finding has directly changed how I respond to silence in my own bilingual classroom.*

## Finding 4: Absence of Shared Institutional Framework

### Academic Wording
No teacher, across questionnaire or interview data, referenced a formal questioning framework or shared school-level guidance on bilingual questioning practice.

### Professional Wording
Identified a systemic gap — every teacher was independently working out their own approach to bilingual questioning, with no shared school policy or framework to draw on.

### CV Wording
*Identified an absence of shared institutional guidance on bilingual questioning practice through systematic research across seven teachers.*

### LinkedIn Wording
One of the clearest findings from my research: not one of seven teachers I surveyed or interviewed could point to any shared school framework for questioning in a bilingual classroom. Everyone was solving the same problem alone.

### Interview Wording
"What struck me most was the absence — nobody I spoke to could point to a shared framework or policy for bilingual questioning. Everyone was managing it individually, through trial and error. That tells you the gap I found earlier isn't really a training problem — it's a systems problem."

### Leadership Wording
Direct evidence for a concrete, low-cost school-improvement action: introducing shared guidance or a simple framework for bilingual questioning practice, rather than relying on individual teachers to solve the same problem in isolation.

### Portfolio Wording
*A structural finding from my research: no teacher in my school referenced any shared framework or policy for questioning in bilingual lessons. Every teacher was managing the same real challenge independently — which suggests professional development focused on technique alone won't be enough without institutional support behind it.*

## Methodological Rigour (Cross-Cutting)

### Academic Wording
Data were triangulated across questionnaire (Likert and open-ended) and interview sources; themes were retained only where evidence appeared across at least two independent sources; reflexive memoing was used throughout to manage insider-researcher positionality.

### Professional Wording
Designed and ran a small-scale mixed-methods study with a documented, auditable analytical process — including an explicit reliability threshold for themes and ongoing reflexive checks on my own bias as an insider researcher.

### CV Wording
*Designed and conducted a mixed-methods practitioner study with triangulated data sources and a documented, auditable coding process (Master's level, 75/100).*

### LinkedIn Wording
Behind every finding in this research is a fairly boring but important discipline: I only kept a theme if it showed up independently in at least two different data sources, and I kept a running log of moments where my own assumptions might have been shaping what I saw. It's not glamorous, but it's what makes the findings defensible.

### Interview Wording
"I was conscious the whole way through that I was researching my own school, so I built in checks — a theme only counted if it appeared in at least two independent sources, and I kept reflexive notes on my own reactions during interviews, including moments where I noticed I might be steering the conversation."

### Leadership Wording
Demonstrates the kind of evidence discipline needed to lead a credible school self-evaluation or data-informed improvement process — not just gathering data, but being explicit about its limits and checking one's own bias.

### Portfolio Wording
*This research was designed with the checks a small-scale, single-school study needs: triangulation across three data sources, a stated reliability threshold for themes, and reflexive memoing throughout to manage my own position as an insider researcher.*
